# Example: usability session excerpt (constructed quality reference)

> Constructed example showing moderation style. Not real data and not evidence
> about any product. Replace with reviewed transcripts when available.

**Context:** grocery app, task about rescheduling a delivery.

**Moderator:** Imagine your grocery delivery is booked for tomorrow morning, but
you've just been asked to work an early shift. Using these screens, show me what
you'd do. Please think out loud as you go.

**Participant:** OK, so this is my home page… I'm looking for my order. There's a
banner about deals, not helpful. I guess I'd tap my profile? No — wait, there's
"Upcoming" down here, I almost missed it. I'd tap that. … Now I see the order,
but I don't see "change time" anywhere. There's "Edit order" — would that let me
change the time, or just the items? I'd probably try it, but I'm a bit worried
it'll make me redo the whole cart.

**Moderator:** What made you hesitate before tapping "Edit order"?

**Participant:** It sounds like it's for the groceries, not the time. If it said
"Change delivery" I'd just tap it.

**Moderator:** How would you rate how easy that was, from 1, very difficult, to 7,
very easy?

**Participant:** Maybe a 3. I got there, but I wasn't sure.

### Why this is good moderation
- Scenario gives a goal and motivation without naming UI labels.
- Moderator waits until the attempt ends, then asks one neutral probe tied to
  an observed hesitation.
- The probe asks about the participant's experience ("What made you hesitate"),
  not about the design team's intent.
