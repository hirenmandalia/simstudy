# Defining personas for simulated participants

## What a useful persona contains
- **Context, goals, motivations, needs, pain points and behaviours**, plus how the person approaches the product and their **experience level** with it [1][2].
- **Only details that would affect a design decision.** Drop the rest [2].
- In this product each persona also records:
  - **Tech savviness and product familiarity:** experience level is a standard persona field [2].
  - **Constraints:** time, money, device, attention, accessibility.
  - **Communication style:** so responses sound like the person rather than a UX expert.
  - **Relevance:** which target-user requirement the persona covers.

## Simulated personas are proto-personas
- **Proto-personas** capture a team's existing knowledge or best guesses without new research. Qualitative personas come from about 5–30 interviews, and statistical personas add surveys and clustering [1].
- SimStudy's personas are proto-personas: assumptions written down. Label them that way. Where possible, base them on real evidence the PM provides, and upgrade to research-based personas once real research exists [1].
- See 07 on why grounding personas in real data matters.

## Behaviour, not demographics
- **Build personas on behaviours, attitudes, goals and pain points,** not demographics such as age, country or income, and not personality traits [3].
- **Demographic details belong only when they drive behaviour.** Names and photos can invite bias [4] (source read via a mirror; check the original before quoting).
- **Disability personas** shouldn't be built on stereotypes or real individuals [5].
- **Never use real or famous people,** identifiable names or real user data. Personas here are fictional.

## Building a set
- **Derive personas from the PM's target-user requirements.** If a segment seems to be missing, suggest it and let the PM decide.
- **Vary the characteristics that plausibly change the answers** to the research questions: experience level, motivation, constraints and accessibility needs. This is our guidance, supported indirectly by [2] and [6].
- **Include range on accessibility.** Include people with different disabilities, and both novice and advanced assistive-tech users. Don't treat one person's input as representative of all disabled people [6]. A "persona spectrum" (permanent, temporary or situational limitations) is a lightweight way to add this [5].
- **Include at least one persona likely to struggle, and a sceptic where relevant.** This is our own guidance, aimed at the over-positivity of simulated participants (see 07).
- **Make personas distinct.** Overlapping personas waste participant slots.

## Avoid
- Stereotypes and personas defined by demographics [3][4][5].
- Personas designed to produce a desired outcome, e.g. all enthusiasts.
- Offensive, abusive or harmful characteristics (product policy).
